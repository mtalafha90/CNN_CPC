\begin{abstract}
Knee magnetic-resonance imaging (MRI) classification is challenging when trusted structured labels are scarce, radiology reports are abundant but incomplete, and each examination contains a heterogeneous set of pulse sequences. We present \code{CNN\_CPC}, a PyTorch framework for leakage-aware weakly supervised, multi-sequence, study-level knee MRI classification. The released development surface contains 4,407 studies and 24,371 MRI series; only 58 studies carry complete labels for the twelve target abnormalities, while 4,349 studies are available primarily through their reports. The pipeline uses competition-only MRI representation learning, multilingual structured report states, six semantic MRI streams, distributed 2.5D ConvNeXt-Tiny features, a cross-sequence Transformer, and twelve interacting pathology queries. A frozen report parser converts each report/target cell to positive, negated, uncertain, or unmentioned; unmentioned findings are never treated as negative. The direct weak-supervision stage uses 3,120 report-only studies containing 14,123 usable target cells and excludes all 58 gold studies from the training gradient and early stopping. Increasing weak-training coverage from approximately 1.28 nominal corpus passes to four complete passes improved the macro ROC AUC from 0.5398 to 0.5645 on the repeatedly used 58-study development set. A subsequent 2x2 spatial-token model decreased macro AUC to 0.5301 and was rejected. A label-free metadata audit then revealed that the historical dual-stream selector placed 552 of 21,886 selected training streams (2.52\%) into a semantic slot inconsistent with the supplied \code{Fluid\_Sensitive} flag. This motivated a predeclared strict-routing experiment in which unavailable contrasts remain masked rather than being replaced by an opposite-contrast sequence. Its predictive evaluation is intentionally not reported until the frozen run is complete. The software includes DICOM metadata repair, geometry-aware slice ordering, robust intensity normalization, full decoding audits, checkpoint provenance, test-time augmentation, bootstrap uncertainty, paired model comparison, and single-GPU runtime protection. The current results are development/model-selection estimates rather than independent hidden-test performance.

{\em Nature of problem:} The task is study-level multi-label classification of twelve knee MRI abnormalities from heterogeneous DICOM examinations. Only a small gold subset provides complete structured labels, whereas most studies provide radiology reports that may omit normal or clinically secondary findings. Treating report silence as a negative label produces systematic label noise. On the imaging side, examinations contain variable sagittal, coronal, and axial acquisitions with different contrast sensitivity, spatial resolution, scanner vendors, and missing sequence types. A useful method must therefore combine weak supervision with explicit sequence semantics, missing-stream handling, leakage control, and practical runtime constraints. \\

{\em Solution method:} CNN\_CPC validates the competition tables, repairs missing MRI-series metadata from DICOM headers when necessary, and represents each study with up to six semantic streams: fluid-sensitive and structural acquisitions for the sagittal, coronal, and axial planes. DICOM slices are physically ordered from orientation/position information, rescaled, MONOCHROME1-corrected, percentile normalized, and sampled as distributed 2.5D triplets. A ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder produces slice features; learned slice and stream embeddings are fused by a Transformer; twelve pathology tokens interact and cross-attend to the MRI memory before target-specific classification. Competition reports are converted into conservative pathology-specific weak labels, with uncertain and unmentioned cells ignored. The main retained model is initialized from a competition-only image--report representation checkpoint and trained for four full passes over all 3,120 active weakly labelled studies using target-balanced weighted binary cross-entropy. \\

{\em Restrictions and unusual features:} Reports are used for training supervision only; final inference is MRI-only. External pretrained image weights are disabled in the conservative competition path. The current 58-study gold set has informed repeated development decisions, so reported scores are explicitly treated as development/model-selection estimates. The final competition leaderboard performance remains unknown until a real submission is evaluated. CNN\_CPC is research software and is not a clinical diagnostic device.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Magnetic-resonance imaging provides complementary information about knee ligaments, menisci, cartilage, bone, synovium, and joint fluid through multiple anatomical planes and pulse sequences. Deep learning has demonstrated the feasibility of examination-level knee MRI classification, with MRNet providing an influential multi-series baseline \cite{bien2018mrnet}. Modern convolutional encoders such as ConvNeXt \cite{liu2022convnext} and attention-based sequence models \cite{vaswani2017attention} make it possible to integrate evidence from many slices and acquisitions. In the present setting, however, network capacity is not the only limiting factor. The central methodological problem is how to learn twelve target classifiers when structured labels are scarce, free-text reports are incomplete, MRI acquisition protocols vary substantially, and the same small gold set must be protected from direct training leakage.

CNN\_CPC was developed around five principles. First, reports are training supervision rather than inference inputs. Second, report silence is not a negative label. Third, all MRI sequence roles and missing acquisitions are represented explicitly. Fourth, the gold studies do not enter the weak-training gradient or early stopping. Fifth, every new development experiment is named and evaluated under a predeclared rule so that post-hoc tuning on the 58 gold studies is visible rather than hidden.

The current repository evolved through a controlled sequence of experiments. Competition-only MRI self-supervision improved the neural baseline; image--report representation alignment produced a stronger frozen representation; structured report states enabled direct pathology-specific weak supervision; complete corpus coverage further improved the retained model; a spatial-token architecture was tested and rejected; and a subsequent label-free metadata audit identified a semantic routing inconsistency that motivated the current strict-routing experiment. This manuscript documents the software and reports the measured development evidence available as of the package-0.14.0 snapshot.

\section{Problem definition and data contract}

For each study $i$, the model predicts
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item ACL,
\item MCL,
\item medial meniscus,
\item lateral meniscus,
\item medial osteoarthritis,
\item lateral osteoarthritis,
\item patellofemoral osteoarthritis,
\item effusion,
\item synovitis,
\item Baker's cyst,
\item contusion, and
\item fracture.
\end{enumerate}

The primary development metric is the macro average of the twelve target-wise ROC AUC values.

\subsection{Series metadata}

Each series row contains at least
\code{StudyInstanceUID}, \code{SeriesInstanceUID}, \code{Fluid\_Sensitive},
\code{Fat\_Suppression}, and \code{Anatomical\_Plane}. The plane is normalized to sagittal, coronal, or axial. Boolean fields are parsed conservatively and may remain unknown until DICOM-based repair is attempted. Populated competition CSV metadata are treated as authoritative; DICOM-derived sequence characterization is a fallback for missing fields rather than a replacement for supplied labels.

\section{DICOM ingestion}
\label{sec:dicom}

\subsection{Series discovery and spatial ordering}

Selected series are discovered under the configured training/test directory hierarchy. Each readable DICOM object is decoded using pydicom. When \code{ImageOrientationPatient} and \code{ImagePositionPatient} are available, the slice normal is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf n=\mathbf r\times\mathbf c,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf c$ are the row and column direction cosines, and each slice receives coordinate
\begin{equation}
z=\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf n.
\end{equation}
Slices are ordered by $z$. \code{InstanceNumber} is used as a fallback. Multi-frame objects are expanded while preserving deterministic frame order.

\subsection{Intensity handling}

Pixel values are converted to floating point and transformed using
\begin{equation}
x'=a x+b,
\end{equation}
where $a$ and $b$ are the DICOM rescale slope and intercept. MONOCHROME1 images are inverted. The finite 1st and 99th percentiles are computed for each volume; intensities are clipped to this interval and normalized:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{x}=
\frac{\operatorname{clip}(x',q_{0.01},q_{0.99})-q_{0.01}}
{\max(q_{0.99}-q_{0.01},10^{-6})}.
\end{equation}
Frames are resized to $224\times224$ by bilinear interpolation after volume normalization.

\section{Semantic MRI stream routing}
\label{sec:routing}

\subsection{Six-stream representation}

The neural model accepts up to six streams:
\begin{align*}
&\text{sagittal-fluid},\quad \text{sagittal-structural},\\
&\text{coronal-fluid},\quad \text{coronal-structural},\\
&\text{axial-fluid},\quad \text{axial-structural}.
\end{align*}
A binary presence mask accompanies every study. Missing streams are represented by zeros and excluded from attention by the mask.

\subsection{Historical dual selector}

The historical B7/B7.1 selector ranked candidate series within each plane using supplied fluid-sensitivity and fat-suppression metadata. When several series were present, it attempted to populate both semantic slots with different acquisitions. This avoided duplicate use of one series but introduced a subtle failure mode: if a plane contained several acquisitions of only one contrast class, the selector could place one of them into the opposite semantic slot.

A label-free audit over all training metadata found 552 semantic mismatches among 21,886 historically selected streams:

\subsection{Strict-routing policy}

The predeclared B9 policy enforces
\begin{align}
\text{fluid slot} &\Rightarrow \code{Fluid\_Sensitive=True},\\
\text{structural slot} &\Rightarrow \code{Fluid\_Sensitive=False}.
\end{align}
If a contrast class is unavailable, the slot remains absent and is masked. Unknown contrast is not silently interpreted as structural. The B7.1 selector remains unchanged in the repository for exact reproducibility; B9 uses a separate strict-routing implementation.

\section{Distributed 2.5D representation}
\label{sec:25d}

For a decoded series of $N$ frames, $S=16$ center positions are distributed approximately uniformly over the valid range. For center $c_s$ and triplet gap $g$,
\begin{equation}
X_s=[I_{c_s-g},I_{c_s},I_{c_s+g}].
\end{equation}
Evaluation uses $g=1$. Training samples $g\in\{1,2\}$ and may jitter the center by up to two slices. The nominal study tensor is therefore
\begin{equation}
[B,K,S,C,H,W]=[B,6,16,3,224,224].
\end{equation}

Training augmentation is deliberately mild: rotation up to $5^\circ$, translation up to 3\%, scale jitter of 5\%, gamma perturbation, low-frequency multiplicative bias, Gaussian noise, and slice dropout. Decoded volumes are cached per DataLoader worker to reduce repeated DICOM I/O.

\section{Competition-only representation learning}
\label{sec:representation}

\subsection{Strong MRI self-supervision}

The strong in-domain MRI self-supervised checkpoint was trained only on the 4,349 non-gold competition studies. It completed eight epochs, 8,000 batches, and 24,000 study draws, corresponding to approximately 5.52 nominal corpus passes. The encoder learned from multiple semantic streams of the same knee and did not use external image weights.

Relative to the random-initialization Stage-1 baseline, the strong SSL initialization improved macro development AUC from 0.4763 to 0.5030.

\subsection{B5 image--report representation alignment}

B5 retained competition-only MRI and aligned image representations with report semantics without using an external language model. Reports were represented using TF--IDF word 1--2 grams followed by truncated SVD and $\ell_2$ normalization. The MRI encoder was initialized from the strong SSL checkpoint and optimized with image--image and image--report alignment objectives.

The completed B5 checkpoint used zero gold studies. A frozen downstream probe yielded
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{macro}}=0.5243650851,
\end{equation}
with 95\% bootstrap interval $[0.4728108406,0.5761619105]$. B5 became the representation initialization used for direct B6-supervised B7/B7.1/B9 training.

\section{Pathology-query MRI classifier}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{ConvNeXt-Tiny slice encoder}

Every active 2.5D triplet is encoded by ConvNeXt-Tiny \cite{liu2022convnext}. External pretrained weights are disabled; B7/B7.1/B9 initialize the encoder from the competition-only B5 checkpoint. The final pooled feature dimension is $d=768$.

For stream $k$ and sampled position $s$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf h_{ks}
=M_k(\mathbf f_{ks}+\mathbf p_s+\mathbf e_k),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf f_{ks}$ is the image feature, $\mathbf p_s$ is a learned slice-position embedding, $\mathbf e_k$ is a learned stream embedding, and $M_k$ is the stream presence mask.

\subsection{Cross-sequence Transformer}

The standard B7.1 MRI memory contains
\begin{equation}
6\times16=96
\end{equation}
tokens. These are processed by a two-layer, eight-head Transformer encoder with pre-normalization, feed-forward width $2d$, and dropout 0.25.

\subsection{Interacting pathology queries}

Twelve learned pathology embeddings
\begin{equation}
Q^{(0)}\in\R^{12\times d}
\end{equation}
first interact through a one-layer Transformer. Multi-head cross-attention then uses the pathology tokens as queries and the MRI memory as keys and values:
\begin{equation}
A=\operatorname{MHA}(Q^{(1)},H,H).
\end{equation}
After residual normalization, each pathology token is mapped to one logit,
\begin{equation}
z_c=\mathbf v_c^\top Q^{(2)}_c+b_c,
\qquad
\hat y_c=\sigmoid(z_c).
\end{equation}

\section{Direct B6-supervised training}
\label{sec:training}

For logits $z_{ic}$, soft weak targets $y_{ic}$, base weights $w_{ic}$, and target-balance multiplier $m_c$, the training loss is
\begin{equation}
L=
\frac{\sum_{i,c}m_c w_{ic}\,
\ell_{\mathrm{BCE}}(z_{ic},y_{ic})}
{\sum_{i,c}m_c w_{ic}}.
\end{equation}
Only positive and negated B6 cells at confidence at least 0.75 contribute.

The strict-routing hypothesis was derived from the label-free metadata audit in Table~\ref{tab:routing}, not from selecting target-wise winners on the gold set. B9 returns to the B7.1 architecture and B5 initialization. The only scientific change is exact \code{Fluid\_Sensitive} routing with no cross-contrast substitution. B6 labels, target balancing, augmentation, learning rates, four full passes, TTA, and bootstrap policy remain unchanged.

At the present manuscript snapshot, B9 training is active and its gold evaluation has not been performed. No B9 AUC is therefore reported. The frozen comparison will be B7.1 versus B9 using the aligned 58 studies and 5,000 paired study-level bootstrap replicates.

\section{Statistical evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

For each target with both classes represented, ROC AUC is computed and the primary score is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{macro}}
=\frac{1}{12}\sum_{c=1}^{12}\mathrm{AUC}_c.
\end{equation}
All twelve target AUCs are defined on the current 58-study gold set.

Uncertainty is estimated with study-level bootstrap resampling. Current B7/B8/B9 development evaluation uses 5,000 replicates. For paired comparison of models $A$ and $B$, the same resampled study indices are applied to both prediction matrices:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_b=
\mathrm{AUC}_{B,b}-\mathrm{AUC}_{A,b}.
\end{equation}
We report the median difference, percentile 95\% interval, and fraction of valid replicates with $\Delta_b>0$.

Because the 58 studies have informed repeated development decisions, these bootstrap intervals quantify sampling variability within the current development surface; they do not restore independent-test status.

\section{Runtime and reproducibility}
\label{sec:runtime}

The conservative execution path uses one GPU and CPU multiprocessing for DICOM/data loading. External image pretraining is disabled. Long experiments use an 8.5-hour software budget with a 10-minute reserve. Checkpoints are written after completed epochs and record variant identity, model specification, source checkpoint provenance, B6 version, supervision policy, training history, and zero-gold-gradient certification.

The principal full-coverage B7.1 experiment completed in approximately 2.05 hours on the verified local GPU environment. B8, with four times as many MRI memory tokens, required approximately 3.14 hours. Runtime is reported as an implementation property rather than a predictive metric.

Random seeds are derived from base seed 2026. Bitwise determinism across all accelerated kernels is not claimed, but experiment variants, supervision contracts, routing policy, and checkpoint identity are explicitly serialized.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiment ladder highlights three recurring lessons. First, representation quality matters: competition-only MRI self-supervision and later image--report alignment both improved the development point estimate over the random baseline. Second, direct pathology-specific weak supervision was more useful than representation alignment alone. B7-v1 improved on B5, and the B7.1 full-coverage correction produced the strongest standalone development point estimate to date. Third, additional architectural complexity is not automatically beneficial. B8 optimized smoothly but generalized worse than the simpler globally pooled B7.1 model.

The routing audit adds a distinct lesson. Semantic stream embeddings are useful only if the upstream acquisition assignment is semantically consistent. A model told that an image is ``axial structural'' while the metadata identify it as fluid-sensitive receives contradictory supervision at the representation level. The historical selector created this contradiction in 552 selected streams. Strict routing deliberately accepts more missing streams in exchange for cleaner semantic identity. Whether that trade-off improves predictive AUC remains an empirical question reserved for B9's frozen evaluation.

The weak-label audit also shows why report supervision must be asymmetric. Negated report findings had high negative predictive value, whereas positive mentions were noisier relative to challenge labels. B7 therefore downweights positive weak cells globally rather than pretending all report-derived labels are equally reliable. Importantly, target-specific weights are not tuned after observing target AUCs.

Several limitations remain. The 58-study development set is very small and has been reused across a long experiment campaign, so apparent improvements may be unstable and selection-biased. B6 is a deterministic report parser and can miss unusual multilingual phrasing or contextual nuance. The six-stream representation remains metadata-driven rather than learned end to end. The 2.5D input does not preserve complete volumetric geometry. Physical pixel spacing is not explicitly normalized before resizing to $224\times224$, leaving scanner/protocol scale variation as a possible domain-shift source. Finally, no current result should be interpreted as a hidden-test or clinical-performance claim.

Future work should prioritize improvements that can be justified without target-wise post-hoc tuning: scanner/physical-scale normalization, stronger competition-only representation learning, robust self-training with independent teachers, pathology-family specialists trained under predeclared rules, denser or adaptive slice sampling, and efficient 3D or hybrid 2.5D/3D representations. External validation and an actual competition submission are necessary to establish generalization beyond the current development surface.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CNN\_CPC provides a reproducible framework for weakly supervised multi-sequence knee MRI classification under severe label scarcity. The retained pipeline combines robust DICOM handling, six semantic MRI streams, distributed 2.5D ConvNeXt features, cross-sequence Transformer fusion, interacting pathology queries, competition-only representation initialization, and conservative structured report supervision. On the repeatedly used 58-study development set, complete weak-corpus coverage produced the current best standalone point estimate, macro ROC AUC 0.5645, while a more complex spatial-token architecture decreased performance to 0.5301 and was rejected.

A subsequent label-free metadata audit exposed cross-contrast sequence substitution in the historical six-stream selector, motivating the current B9 strict-routing experiment. B9 is deliberately evaluated as a separate predeclared development variant, and no predictive result is claimed until its frozen training and gold evaluation are complete. This separation between measured evidence, active hypotheses, and unresolved validation is a central design principle of the software.

\section{Current experiment reporting checklist}
\label{app:checklist}

Before promoting a new candidate:
\begin{enumerate}
\item verify the exact experiment variant and checkpoint provenance;
\item confirm that all predefined epochs completed without budget limiting;
\item confirm the expected number of study draws and usable weak cells per epoch;
\item verify that no gold studies entered the gradient or early stopping;
\item inspect the routing audit and metadata-repair records;
\item run the predeclared TTA policy without target-specific adjustment;
\item compute the macro AUC and target-wise AUCs on aligned gold studies;
\item report study-level bootstrap uncertainty;
\item use paired bootstrap for direct candidate comparisons;
\item do not select target-specific post-hoc winners;
\item do not optimize ensemble weights on the same 58 studies;
\item label all current 58-study scores as development/model-selection estimates;
\item separate any future leaderboard score from the development table.
\end{enumerate}
