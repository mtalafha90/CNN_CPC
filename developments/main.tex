\begin{abstract}
Knee magnetic-resonance imaging (MRI) classification is difficult when trusted structured labels are scarce, radiology reports are abundant but incomplete, and each examination contains a heterogeneous set of pulse sequences. We present \code{CNN\_CPC}, a PyTorch framework for leakage-aware weakly supervised, study-level classification of twelve knee abnormalities. The released development surface contains 4,407 studies and 24,371 MRI series; 58 studies carry complete expert labels, while the remaining 4,349 studies are primarily supervised through their reports. A frozen report parser converts each report/target cell to positive, negated, uncertain, or unmentioned, and report silence is never treated as a negative label. The active B20 model uses 3,120 report-supervised studies, 17,475 eligible MRI series, and 14,123 usable target cells. Each real MRI series is represented by sixteen distributed 2.5D slice triplets, a frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder, learned attention pooling to one token per series, study-level Transformer context, and twelve learned pathology queries. B20 additionally applies a deterministic centered 90\% crop to the resized MRI input and resizes back to $224\times224$.

Because the same 58 expert studies were repeatedly used during development, later architecture experiments are reported as reused-development diagnostics rather than independent validation. On this surface, B20 reached macro ROC AUC 0.66741. B29 added a zero-gated second learned series summary and reached 0.67689; B31 added local through-plane context to the complementary scorer and reached 0.68228; B32 added a non-redundant weighted dispersion summary but fell to 0.66870; and B33 removed the learned complementary query entirely, using an exact uniform mean behind a 768-parameter gate, and reached 0.67645. B33 was therefore practically indistinguishable from B29 on the reused surface despite using half as many new parameters, supporting the interpretation that the useful signal is primarily a second broad global series representation rather than learned slice selection. B31 remains the highest reused-development candidate, but no post-B20 candidate is promoted because this surface is not independent.

To stop further architecture optimization against the reused 58-study set, we prospectively froze an 80/20 StudyInstanceUID hash split of the 3,120 active weak-supervision studies before B34. The resulting 2,496-study training partition contains 13,931 eligible series and 11,303 usable weak cells; the untouched 624-study partition is reserved for downstream architecture selection with the exact same frozen B16 encoder. Matched fixed-E2 B20, B31, and B33 controls have completed training. Their epoch-2 training losses are 0.69249, 0.69122, and 0.68335, respectively; these training losses are not selection metrics. The prospectively frozen primary validation metric is the macro average of per-target B6-weighted soft-label binary cross-entropy, with study-level paired bootstrap uncertainty. At the present manuscript snapshot the final 624-study prospective evaluation is still running and no PV1 model ranking is reported. This separation between frozen architecture design, reused expert diagnostics, prospective weak-label architecture selection, and future independent hidden evaluation is a central methodological contribution of the software.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Magnetic-resonance imaging provides complementary information about knee ligaments, menisci, cartilage, bone, synovium, and joint fluid through multiple anatomical planes and pulse sequences. Deep learning has demonstrated the feasibility of examination-level knee MRI classification, with MRNet providing an influential multi-series baseline \cite{bien2018mrnet}. Modern convolutional encoders such as ConvNeXt \cite{liu2022convnext} and attention-based sequence models \cite{vaswani2017attention} make it possible to integrate evidence from many slices and acquisitions. In the present setting, however, network capacity is not the only limiting factor. The central methodological problem is how to learn twelve target classifiers when structured labels are scarce, free-text reports are incomplete, MRI acquisition protocols vary substantially, and a very small expert-labelled set must be protected from direct training leakage and from excessive sequential reuse.

CNN\_CPC was developed around six principles. First, reports are training supervision rather than inference inputs. Second, report silence is not a negative label. Third, all eligible MRI series can contribute evidence instead of forcing each examination into one fixed protocol template. Fourth, expert-labelled studies do not enter weak-training gradients. Fifth, every development change is named and frozen before its evaluation. Sixth, the role of each evaluation surface is recorded explicitly: repeated expert-set results are development diagnostics, prospectively frozen weak-label partitions are architecture-selection surfaces only, and independent hidden or external data are required for promotion claims.

The repository contains a long controlled experiment ladder. Early B0--B9 work established competition-only representation learning, report-derived weak supervision, full weak-corpus coverage, DICOM handling, and several negative ablations. Later B10--B20 work replaced the earlier fixed six-stream abstraction with variable all-series hierarchical aggregation, improved the representation-learning path, and culminated in B20, which remains the active working model. B26--B33 then investigated report-supervision repair and increasingly constrained complementary series-level summaries. These later experiments produced a mechanistic result that now drives the next stage: a very small zero-gated global residual can help B20 on the reused development surface, while additional routing, max-evidence, projected-attention, or dispersion complexity has not provided reliable evidence of further benefit.

\section{Problem definition and data contract}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Targets}
For each study $i$, the model predicts $\hat{\mathbf y}_i\in[0,1]^{12}$ for ACL, MCL, medial meniscus, lateral meniscus, medial osteoarthritis, lateral osteoarthritis, patellofemoral osteoarthritis, effusion, synovitis, Baker's cyst, contusion, and fracture.

\subsection{Released data surface}
The verified release contains 4,407 training studies, 58 fully labelled expert studies, 4,349 report-only studies, and 24,371 series rows. The report-only pool supports representation learning and weak image supervision. The current B20-family supervision surface contains 3,120 active studies with at least one usable B6 target cell. Across those studies, 17,475 MRI series satisfy the frozen variable-series policy and 14,123 target cells are usable for direct weak supervision: 6,871 positive and 7,252 negated.

\subsection{Evaluation roles}
The 58 expert studies were repeatedly used to select or reject historical models. They are therefore referred to throughout this manuscript as the \emph{reused expert development surface}, not as independent validation. The historical 623-study weak-v2 subset is also not a holdout for B20-family experiments because those experiments train on the full 3,120-study active weak-supervision population.

A separate prospective weak-validation framework was frozen on 16 August 2026 before B34. It uses a StudyInstanceUID-only hash split of the 3,120 active studies. This split is fresh with respect to downstream B20--B33 supervised architecture choices, but it is not independent clinical validation and it is not valid for encoder selection because the historical B16 encoder had already been aligned on all 4,349 non-gold MRI/report pairs.

\section{DICOM ingestion and 2.5D preprocessing}
\label{sec:dicom}

Selected DICOM objects are decoded with pydicom. When \code{ImageOrientationPatient} and \code{ImagePositionPatient} are available, slice order is determined from the image-plane normal
\begin{equation}
\mathbf n=\mathbf r\times\mathbf c,
\end{equation}
and scalar coordinate $z=\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf n$. \code{InstanceNumber} is used as fallback. Multi-frame objects are expanded deterministically. Pixel values are rescaled using the DICOM slope and intercept, MONOCHROME1 is inverted, finite 1st and 99th percentiles are used for clipping, and the result is normalized to $[0,1]$ before neural preprocessing.

For each eligible MRI series, sixteen center positions are distributed through the volume. With triplet gap $g$, center $c_s$ produces the 2.5D input
\begin{equation}
X_s=[I_{c_s-g},I_{c_s},I_{c_s+g}].
\end{equation}
Training can vary the gap and center position according to the frozen augmentation policy; evaluation uses the frozen three-center-offset TTA $[-1,0,1]$. Each triplet is represented as three input channels and resized to $224\times224$.

The B20 spatial intervention is intentionally simple. After the historical dataset preprocessing has produced the resized input, B20 takes a deterministic centered crop retaining 90\% of the in-plane extent and resizes the crop back to $224\times224$. No cosine vignette or artificial dark border is applied.

\section{Structured weak supervision from reports}
\label{sec:reports}

For each target, the frozen B6 v1.2.1 parser assigns
\begin{equation}
r_{ic}\in\{\mathrm{positive},\mathrm{negated},\mathrm{uncertain},\mathrm{unmentioned}\}.
\end{equation}
Uncertain and unmentioned cells are ignored. Positive and negated cells use the globally frozen asymmetric policy
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
B6 state & Soft target & Base weight \\
\midrule
positive & 0.85 & 0.50 \\
negated & 0.05 & 1.00 \\
uncertain & ignored & 0.00 \\
unmentioned & ignored & 0.00 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Target-wise multipliers equalize total weak-supervision mass across the twelve outputs. The full active B6 surface contains 14,123 usable cells. Synovitis remains particularly imbalanced in the frozen report supervision, with 399 positive and only 17 negated cells on the full active surface; target-specific architectural decisions are therefore not made from Synovitis outcomes.

\section{B20 hierarchical all-series model}
\label{sec:b20model}

The active B20 model processes every eligible real MRI series in the study under the frozen B12/B13 variable-series policy. For a series with sixteen sampled triplets, the frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder produces slice tokens $X\in\R^{16\times768}$. Historical learned multi-head attention pooling maps these slice tokens to one series representation $A\in\R^{768}$. All real series tokens for the study are then integrated by the historical study-level Transformer, after which twelve learned pathology queries interact and produce twelve logits.

The active computation can be summarized as
\begin{equation}
\text{study}\rightarrow\{\text{real series}\}
\rightarrow\{16\text{ triplets/series}\}
\rightarrow\text{ConvNeXt-Tiny}
\rightarrow A_{\mathrm{series}}
\rightarrow\text{study Transformer}
\rightarrow\text{12 pathology queries}.
\end{equation}

The visual encoder is the historical B15/B16 report-aligned encoder and remains frozen in B20-family downstream experiments. Its canonical SHA-256 fingerprint is
\begin{verbatim}
b328667cf9dfa9b909ef181c1bcc8975ec42bcd8b9eddad08f908875b73fae96
\end{verbatim}
The canonical B20 checkpoint is \code{runs/b20\_crop\_focus/b20\_model.pt}, selected epoch 2. Historical post-B20 development evidence does not automatically replace this checkpoint.

\section{Development governance}
\label{sec:governance}

The current development policy distinguishes three forms of evidence. Reused expert-set AUC is diagnostic evidence only. A prospectively frozen weak-label partition may guide downstream architecture design only when the exact same frozen encoder is shared. Promotion of the active model requires a stronger independent signal, such as hidden competition evaluation or an external expert-labelled dataset.

No candidate may be modified after its reused-expert outcome to exploit target-specific winners. In particular, the project forbids outcome-derived target switching, target-specific gates, selective B20/candidate substitution, post-hoc blend weights, or a ``.1'' variant whose design is motivated by the reused 58-study result. A failed formulation is closed rather than locally optimized against the reused set.

\section{B20-family complementary-series experiments}
\label{sec:b20family}

\subsection{Reference performance and supervision repair}
On the reused 58-study expert surface, the canonical B20 model reaches
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{B20}}=0.6674066371.
\end{equation}
B24X/B25X supervision pilots suggested that broad report-label filling could improve some coverage statistics but also exposed severe Synovitis imbalance. B26.2 then tested a deterministic whitelist repair. Under the matched fixed-E2 comparison B26.2 reached 0.6662972 versus B20 0.6674066, a raw difference of $-0.0011094$ with paired 95\% interval approximately $[-0.01566,0.01425]$. The supervision-repair family was therefore closed.

\subsection{B27.1 metadata routing}
B27.1 added pathology-specific routing based on plane and paired-sequence metadata after an earlier fluid/fat formulation was found to be collinear on the complete 17,475-series surface. B27.1 introduced only 60 trainable routing parameters and optimized successfully, but its reused-expert macro was approximately 0.65992, below B20 by about 0.00748. The routing family was closed and no B27.2 was created.

\subsection{B28 max-evidence residual}
B28 tested whether the elementwise maximum over the sixteen image-content slice embeddings provided complementary evidence to B20's learned series token. With $M$ the elementwise maximum and $A$ the historical B20 token,
\begin{equation}
T=A+\tanh(g)\odot\mathrm{LN}_0(M),
\end{equation}
where $g\in\R^{768}$ was zero initialized and $\mathrm{LN}_0$ denotes parameter-free layer normalization. The gate preserved exact B20 behavior at initialization. B28 reached approximately 0.63835 on the reused expert surface, a raw decline of about 0.02906 from B20. The max-evidence formulation was closed.

\subsection{B29 learned complementary mean-like summary}
B29 introduced a second learned softmax summary over the same sixteen B20 slice tokens. Let
\begin{equation}
C=\mathrm{LN}_0\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{16}w_iX_i\right),\qquad
w_i=\frac{\exp(q^TX_i/\sqrt{768})}{\sum_j\exp(q^TX_j/\sqrt{768})},
\end{equation}
with learned query $q\in\R^{768}$. The final series token is
\begin{equation}
T=A+\tanh(g)\odot(C-A).
\label{eq:b29}
\end{equation}
The learned query and feature-wise gate contribute 1,536 new parameters. The gate is exactly zero initialized, so B29 begins as the exact B20 function.

The fixed-E2 run covered all 3,120 studies, 17,475 series, and 14,123 weak cells. The final effective gate remained small, with maximum absolute value 0.02250 and mean absolute value 0.00516. On the reused 58-study expert surface,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{B29}}=0.6768879224,
\end{equation}
with raw difference $+0.0094813$ from B20. The paired median difference was $+0.0094214$, 95\% interval $[-0.0037494,0.0241876]$, and $P(\mathrm{B29}>\mathrm{B20})=0.9188$. B29 is therefore a frozen promising candidate, but not promoted.

\subsection{B30 projected complementary attention}
B30 tested a more sophisticated complementary attention path using the historical B20 attention projections while detaching the complementary branch from shared parameters. The mechanism was valid and trained, but the reused-expert macro fell to approximately 0.65470, with raw B30--B20 difference $-0.01270$ and $P(\mathrm{B30}>\mathrm{B20})=0.1422$. The projected-attention formulation was closed.

\subsection{B31 local-context complementary scorer}
B31 retained the B29 query but allowed immediate through-plane neighbours to perturb only the complementary attention scores. For the original slice tokens $X$, define
\begin{equation}
H=X+\mathrm{DWConv}_{k=3}(\mathrm{LN}_0(X)),
\end{equation}
where the depthwise Conv1d has 768 groups, kernel size three, no bias, and exact zero initialization. The B29 query scores $H$, while the weighted value sum still uses the original $X$. B31 therefore adds 2,304 local-context parameters on top of B29, for 3,840 new parameters relative to B20.

The fixed-E2 training run was valid. Its prospective mechanism audit showed that the local-context pathway changed the B29 attention distribution only minimally: normalized Jensen--Shannon divergence was approximately $3.56\times10^{-9}$, top-1 slice agreement was 98.9\%, and top-3 overlap was approximately 99.63\%. The effective residual remained small. Nevertheless, on the reused expert surface B31 reached
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{B31}}=0.6822797439,
\end{equation}
the highest value in the current reused-development lineage. Relative to B20 the raw difference is $+0.0148731$; relative to B29 it is $+0.0053918$. The paired intervals remain broad, so B31 is a frozen leading development candidate rather than a promoted model. The small attention redistribution also means that the causal mechanism behind its numerical gain is uncertain.

\subsection{B32 weighted dispersion summary}
B32 tested whether second-order through-series information could add something genuinely distinct from the B29 mean-like residual. Using the same B29 attention weights,
\begin{align}
\mu&=\sum_iw_iX_i,\\
\sigma&=\sqrt{\sum_iw_i(X_i-\mu)^2+10^{-6}},
\end{align}
and the final token was
\begin{equation}
T=A+\tanh(g_\mu)\odot(\mathrm{LN}_0(\mu)-A)
+\tanh(g_\sigma)\odot\mathrm{LN}_0(\sigma).
\end{equation}
The prospective mechanism audit confirmed that the dispersion residual was non-redundant: the mean--dispersion residual cosine was approximately 0.074 at E2. However, non-redundancy was not sufficient for performance. B32 reached 0.6686993213, only $+0.0012927$ above B20 and below both B29 and B31. The paired median B32--B31 difference was $-0.0131667$, 95\% interval $[-0.0373309,0.0066110]$, with $P(\mathrm{B32}>\mathrm{B31})=0.0946$. The weighted-dispersion formulation is closed.

\subsection{B33 exact uniform complementary mean}
B33 was designed as the simplest direct test of the mechanism suggested by B29 and B31: whether a second broad series representation is useful even without learned complementary slice selection. It removes the B29 query entirely and defines
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{mean}}=\mathrm{LN}_0\!\left(\frac{1}{16}\sum_{i=1}^{16}X_i\right),
\end{equation}
with final token
\begin{equation}
T=A+\tanh(g)\odot(C_{\mathrm{mean}}-A).
\label{eq:b33}
\end{equation}
Only the 768-dimensional zero-initialized feature-wise gate is new.

On the full 3,120-study fixed-E2 run, the B33 gate received non-zero gradients and remained conservative. The final mean absolute effective gate was approximately 0.00502 and the maximum approximately 0.02290. The mechanism audit showed that $C_{\mathrm{mean}}$ and $A$ were almost orthogonal, with cosine approximately $-0.0023$, while the effective injected residual remained only about 0.64\% of the B20 token norm on average. Thus B33 learns to introduce a very small amount of a substantially different global representation.

On the reused expert surface,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{B33}}=0.6764460785.
\end{equation}
This is only $-0.0004418$ below B29. The paired B33--B29 median difference was $-0.0004964$, 95\% interval $[-0.0233115,0.0220359]$, with $P(\mathrm{B33}>\mathrm{B29})=0.4800$. B33 is therefore practically indistinguishable from B29 at the resolution of this reused surface despite using half as many new parameters. This supports, but does not independently prove, the interpretation that B29's useful signal comes primarily from a second global series representation rather than learned non-uniform slice selection.

\section{Prospective weak-validation v1}
\label{sec:pv1}

\subsection{Reason for creating a new surface}
By B33 the 58-study expert set had been inspected across many architecture decisions. Continuing to design candidates against it would increasingly optimize to the evaluation set. The project therefore retired it as the primary architecture-design surface and froze prospective weak-validation v1 (PV1) before any B34 experiment.

\subsection{Frozen split}
PV1 assigns studies using only StudyInstanceUID. For UID $u$,
\begin{equation}
k(u)=\mathrm{SHA256}(\texttt{CNN\_CPC|prospective-weak-v1|2026-08-16}\,\Vert\,\texttt{NUL}\,\Vert\,u).
\end{equation}
All 3,120 active UIDs are sorted by $k(u)$. The first 624 form validation and the remaining 2,496 form training. B6 states, weak-label confidences, expert labels, previous model scores, and model predictions do not influence membership. The frozen split fingerprint is
\begin{verbatim}
a0032307abb1ab99724eb39fac25332ce131c575f64d823083bb37f5ec20d1e6
\end{verbatim}
This certifies StudyInstanceUID-level separation only; patient-identity separation is not claimed because a trustworthy patient identifier is not part of the frozen supervision contract.

The training partition contains 13,931 eligible MRI series and 11,303 usable weak cells, of which 5,559 are positive and 5,744 negated. The validation partition contains 624 studies and remains excluded from the supervised matched-control gradient and checkpoint selection.

\subsection{Representation-pretraining limitation}
The historical B16 encoder was aligned using all 4,349 non-gold MRI/report pairs before PV1 was conceived. Therefore the 624 PV1 validation studies are not unseen by the encoder. PV1 is valid only for comparing downstream architecture changes that share the exact same frozen B16 encoder fingerprint. It must not be used to select a new encoder, representation-pretraining method, or any method that retrains the encoder using this population.

\subsection{Matched controls}
Before B34, B20, B31, and B33 were retrained from the same historical B16/B20 initialization on exactly the 2,496-study PV1 training subset. All runs use the same split, frozen encoder, 90\% B20 crop, variable-series policy, optimizer, learning rate, augmentation, loader seed, five-epoch scheduler horizon, and fixed E2 endpoint. The trainer also resets the post-construction stochastic seed after model creation so that extra B31/B33 parameter initialization does not shift the downstream dropout/random-training path.

All three audits report construction seed 19,002,026, loader seed 19,102,026, and post-construction training seed 19,202,026. No validation study or expert study enters the gradient. The frozen encoder fingerprint remains unchanged before and after each run.

\subsection{Frozen primary and secondary metrics}
The primary PV1 metric is the macro average of per-target B6-weighted soft-label binary cross-entropy. For target $c$,
\begin{equation}
L_c=\frac{\sum_i w_{ic}\left[-y_{ic}\log p_{ic}-(1-y_{ic})\log(1-p_{ic})\right]}
{\sum_i w_{ic}},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{PV1}}=\frac{1}{12}\sum_{c=1}^{12}L_c,
\end{equation}
where lower is better. The secondary metric is macro ROC AUC over hard B6 positive/negated states for targets where both classes are present. Paired study-level bootstrap differences of the primary loss are predeclared for B31--B20, B33--B20, and B33--B31.

\subsection{Current PV1 evaluation status}
The first attempt to evaluate the three PV1 checkpoints simultaneously was terminated by the operating system's memory-pressure manager before any scientific comparison was accepted. The GNOME terminal scope reached a recorded 56.6~GB memory peak and 3.5~GB swap peak, after which \code{systemd-oomd} killed the process group. This failure is treated as an execution defect, not a model result.

The evaluator was subsequently changed from version 1.0.0 to a memory-conservative version 1.1.0. It now loads one checkpoint at a time, predicts all 624 validation studies, stores predictions on CPU/disk, deletes the model and checkpoint payload, runs garbage collection and CUDA cache release, and only then loads the next checkpoint. Evaluation is additionally frozen to batch size one, one DataLoader worker, prefetch factor one, no persistent worker, and zero worker-local raw-series cache. The scientific TTA offsets $[-1,0,1]$, checkpoint identities, prediction semantics, primary metric, secondary metric, and bootstrap definitions are unchanged.

At the time of this manuscript update, the corrected low-memory PV1 evaluation is running. No B20/B31/B33 PV1 ranking is therefore reported here. This is deliberate: the manuscript records only completed results and does not infer validation performance from the matched-control training losses.

\section{Runtime and execution policy}
\label{sec:runtime}

Competition mode uses one requested GPU with CPU multiprocessing for DICOM/data work. Current B20-family full-surface candidate runs use a hard internal cap of 8.25 h and reserve at least 30 min below the 9 h competition inference/runtime ceiling. Recent B29--B33 fixed-E2 training runs complete in roughly one hour on the NVIDIA RTX A4500 Laptop GPU used for local experiments. The PV1 matched-control runs complete in approximately 47--53 min each because only 80\% of the active weak-supervision studies are used.

Runtime controls are separated from scientific controls. Reducing evaluation batch size, worker count, prefetch depth, or cache size is allowed when it does not change MRI sampling, TTA, checkpoint weights, or prediction arithmetic. The confirmed PV1 out-of-memory event motivated such a resource-only change and is now explicitly documented for reproducibility.

\section{Reproducibility and artifacts}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

The repository preserves the full B0--B33 development archive under \code{developments/} while exposing B20 as the clean top-level current model. Every recent candidate records its architecture specification, frozen supervision contract, encoder fingerprint, crop policy, fixed endpoint, candidate parameter count, training coverage, gradient-coupling checks, runtime, and whether expert or validation labels entered training or checkpoint selection.

PV1 additionally stores \code{split\_manifest.json}, separate matched-control checkpoints and training audits, per-model prediction files, a paired prediction table, and final \code{comparison.json}. The split manifest contains the exact training and validation UIDs plus SHA-256 fingerprints. Once generated, the split cannot be silently altered after outcomes are inspected.

The repository also records explicit decision status. B20 remains active. B29 is a frozen promising candidate. B31 is the numerically strongest reused-development candidate but is not independently validated. B32's dispersion formulation is closed. B33 is a successful frozen simplification of B29. No B34 is to be designed from PV1 outcomes until the predeclared B20/B31/B33 matched comparison completes.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The full development history supports several conclusions. First, representation quality and report-derived supervision both matter: the early B0--B7 progression showed that competition-only image learning, report alignment, and direct pathology-specific weak supervision each provided useful steps. Second, the negative B8, B9, B27.1, B28, B30, and B32 experiments show repeatedly that increased structural sophistication or mechanistic novelty is not sufficient for improved ranking. Third, the B29--B33 sequence isolates a much simpler useful pattern than originally expected.

B29 suggested that a second zero-gated series representation can complement B20. B31 achieved the highest reused-expert macro, but its local-context branch barely changed the complementary attention distribution, weakening a direct causal claim that through-plane neighbour context produced the gain. B32 then demonstrated that a genuinely non-redundant second-order dispersion residual still failed to improve the result consistently. Finally, B33 removed the learned complementary query and recovered essentially the same reused-expert macro as B29 with only 768 new parameters. Taken together, the most economical current hypothesis is that the B20 series token benefits from a tiny residual contribution from a second broad global representation; neither learned non-uniform slice selection nor second-order dispersion has yet been shown to be necessary.

This interpretation must remain proportional to the evidence. All B20--B33 AUC comparisons above come from the repeatedly reused 58-study surface. B31's numerical lead, B29/B33's apparent benefit, and B32's failure are useful development observations but cannot by themselves authorize promotion. The creation of PV1 is therefore not merely an engineering convenience; it is a methodological response to accumulated evaluation reuse.

PV1 itself also has limits. Its labels are still produced by B6, and the historical B16 encoder saw the validation reports during representation alignment. Thus PV1 measures downstream architecture behavior under a shared frozen representation, not independent clinical generalization. It is stronger for B34 architecture selection than returning again to the 58-study set, but hidden competition or new expert-labelled studies remain necessary for a definitive model replacement claim.

Weak-label reliability remains another limitation. The B6 parser is deterministic and report language is incomplete. Synovitis has extremely few explicit negated cells, which makes both training and weak-state validation particularly fragile for that target. This is one reason the PV1 primary metric is a predeclared macro weighted soft-label loss rather than target-wise winner selection.

Other limitations include the use of sixteen sampled 2.5D positions rather than full three-dimensional volumes, scanner and physical-scale heterogeneity, the lack of certified patient-level separation in PV1, and the absence of an independent hidden-test result in the current manuscript snapshot.

\section{Future directions}
\label{sec:future}

The immediate next step is not another architecture. The corrected PV1 evaluation must first complete and establish the matched B20/B31/B33 downstream baseline on the prospectively frozen 624-study partition. Only then should B34 be designed, and B34 should preserve the exact shared frozen B16 encoder if PV1 is used for selection.

If B33 matches or exceeds B31 prospectively, the preferred direction is to build from the simpler global residual in Eq.~\eqref{eq:b33}, because it has a clearer mechanism and far fewer new parameters. If B31 remains superior prospectively, follow-up work should investigate its optimization dynamics without using the old 58-study target outcomes to choose kernels, targets, or blends. If neither candidate improves B20 on PV1, the complementary-series family should be reconsidered rather than tuned to force a gain.

Longer-term directions include physically normalized in-plane sampling using DICOM \code{PixelSpacing}; acquisition-set models that preserve multiple same-plane series without semantic slot forcing; stronger report supervision or uncertainty calibration; full-volume or adaptive slice sampling for focal abnormalities; and an external expert-labelled validation cohort. Any new encoder or representation-learning experiment requires a separately frozen evaluation surface because PV1 cannot validly select encoder changes.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CNN\_CPC has evolved from a fixed-stream weakly supervised MRI classifier into a leakage-aware hierarchical all-series framework with explicit experiment governance. The active B20 model uses a frozen report-aligned ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder, sixteen 2.5D slice positions per real MRI series, learned series pooling, study-level Transformer context, twelve pathology queries, and a deterministic 90\% center-crop intervention. On the repeatedly reused 58-study expert development surface B20 reaches macro AUC 0.66741, but that number is not treated as independent validation.

The post-B20 complementary-series experiments narrow the architecture question substantially. B29 reaches 0.67689 with a learned complementary summary; B31 reaches 0.68228 with local-context scoring; B32's non-redundant dispersion branch falls to 0.66870; and B33 reaches 0.67645 using only an exact uniform mean and one 768-parameter zero-gated residual. B33's near-equivalence to B29 indicates that the learned complementary query is not necessary on the reused development surface. B31 remains numerically highest there, but the mechanism audit shows almost no attention redistribution and the result is not independent.

The principal current methodological advance is therefore the transition away from repeated expert-set optimization. Prospective weak-validation v1 freezes a StudyInstanceUID-only 80/20 split before B34, retrains matched B20/B31/B33 controls on 2,496 studies, and reserves 624 studies for a predeclared downstream comparison under the exact shared frozen B16 encoder. All matched-control training audits are valid. The corrected low-memory final evaluation is in progress at this manuscript snapshot, so no prospective winner is yet claimed. B20 remains the active model until stronger evidence supports promotion.
